The Critical Rule in Factorial ANOVA Lab Reports

When an Interaction is Significant,
Main Effects Are Qualified.

Never interpret or run post-hoc tests on marginal main effects as if they tell the whole story. A significant interaction means that the effect of Factor A depends on the level of Factor B. Decompose the omnibus outcome into simple effects and report the exact test statistics including pairwise t-ratios, degrees of freedom, exact p-values, means, and standard deviations.

2-Way Interaction (e.g., Factor A × Factor C)

Decompose into Simple Main Effects: Test the effect of one factor at each single level of the second factor (e.g., comparing Factor C levels within Level 1 of A, Level 2 of A, and Level 3 of A).

3-Way Interaction (e.g., A × B × C)

Decompose into Simple Simple Effects: Test the effect of Factor A across the four combinations of Factors B and C (e.g., Political Orientation compared within Non-Voters with Kids, Voters with Kids, etc.).

Scenario: 2-Way Interaction is Significant

Decompose into Simple Main Effects

Main effects are qualified. Interpret simple effects across slices.
R Syntax (`emmeans`) 
# Fit the linear model
model <- lm(Score ~ FactorA * FactorC, data = my_data)

# Decompose interaction: Test Factor C at each level of Factor A
emmeans(model, pairwise ~ FactorC | FactorA, adjust = "none")$contrasts
Correct APA 7 Lab Reporting Template Strict APA Standard
Because the main effect of Factor A was qualified by the Factor A × Factor C interaction, simple effects of Factor C were evaluated at each level of Factor A. At Level 1 of Factor A, participants in Level 1 of Factor C (M = 2.94, SD = 0.35) scored significantly higher than those in Level 2 (M = 2.61, SD = 0.31), t(48) = 3.35, p = .001. Conversely, the simple effect of Factor C was non-significant at Level 2, t(48) = 0.43, p = .672, and at Level 3, t(48) = 0.64, p = .525.
Instructor's Lab Guidance:

Notice that we report the test for every level of Factor A (Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3). Each pairwise contrast includes its t-statistic, degrees of freedom, exact p-value, and both condition means with standard deviations.

Reference Corner

APA 7th Edition Rules Cheat Sheet

Keep these formatting and reporting standards handy when proofreading your lab submissions.

01

Full Test Statistics on Contrasts

Never report only means and p-values for pairwise tests. State the test statistic with degrees of freedom: t(48) = 3.35, p = .001. This anchors the comparison in the model error term.

02

No Leading Zeros & Exact P

Omit the leading zero for quantities capped at 1.0 (p = .042 and ηp2 = .18). Provide exact values to 2 or 3 decimals rather than ranges like p < .05, reserving p < .001 for values below .001.

03

Always Pair M with SD

Every cell mean must be paired with its dispersion: (M = 3.25, SD = 0.42). Reporting standalone means provides an incomplete descriptive summary in APA style.
