The #1 Mistake in Factorial ANOVA Lab Reports

When an Interaction is Significant,
Main Effects Are Qualified.

Never interpret or run global post-hoc tests on marginal main effects as if they tell the whole story. A significant interaction means that the effect of Factor A changes depending on the level of Factor B. To report this accurately, you must slice the data into conditional simple effects.

2-Way Interaction (e.g., Factor A × Factor C)

Decompose into Simple Main Effects: Test the effect of one factor at each single level of the second factor (e.g., comparing Factor C levels within Level 1 of A, Level 2 of A, and Level 3 of A).

3-Way Interaction (e.g., A × B × C)

Decompose into Simple Simple Effects: Test the effect of Factor A across the four combinations of Factors B and C (e.g., Political Orientation compared within Non-Voters with Kids, Voters without Kids, etc.).

Measurements of our OUTCOMEVARIABLE were taken at three levels of FACTOR A (one, two, and three), two levels of FACTOR B (one and two), and two levels of FACTOR C (one and two) resulting in a 3x2x2 experimental design. Scenario: 2-Way Interaction is Significant

Decompose into Simple Main Effects

Main effects are qualified. Interpret simple effects across slices.
R Syntax (`emmeans`) 
# Fit the linear model
model <- lm(Score ~ FactorA * FactorC, data = my_data)

# Decompose interaction: Test Factor C at each level of Factor A
emmeans(model, pairwise ~ FactorC | FactorA, adjust = "none")$contrasts
Correct APA 7 Lab Reporting Template Strict APA Standard
Because the main effect of Factor A was qualified by the Factor A × Factor C interaction, simple effects of Factor C were evaluated at each level of Factor A. At Level 1 of Factor A, participants in Level 1 of Factor C (M = 2.94, SD = 0.35) scored significantly higher than those in Level 2 (M = 2.61, SD = 0.31), t(48) = 3.35, p = .001. Conversely, the effect of Factor C was non-significant at Level 2 (p = .672) and Level 3 (p = .525).
Instructor's Lab Guidance:

Notice that we report the test for every level of Factor A (Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3). Stopping after finding one significant level is a fatal grading error! All slices must be reported.

Reference Corner

APA 7th Edition Rules Cheat Sheet

Keep these formatting rules handy when proofreading your lab submissions.

01

No Leading Zeros

Do not put a zero before decimals for numbers that cannot exceed 1.0. This applies directly to p values (p = .042, not p = 0.042) and partial eta squared (ηp2 = .18).

02

Exact Probabilities

Report exact p values to 2 or 3 decimal places (e.g., p = .004, p = .088). Never report arbitrary ranges like p < .05 or p < .005. The only exception is values smaller than .001, which are reported as p < .001.

03

Always Report SDs

Every reported cell or marginal mean must be accompanied by its standard deviation or standard error in parentheses: (M = 3.25, SD = 0.42). Standalone means are incomplete statistical summaries in APA format.
