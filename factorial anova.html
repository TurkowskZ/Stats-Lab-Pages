Factorial ANOVA (2 × 5 Between Subjects)

Human Factors & Human-Computer Interaction Lab

Research Question & Design Architecture

Do display characteristics (screen resolution and ambient lighting intensity) and their potential interaction produce significant differences in player difficulty, quantified by the number of lives used up in a video game?

Rows Factor

Graphics Display Resolution

5 Levels (\(N_{\text{Rows}} = 5\))

  • 1: QVGA (320 × 240 pixels)
  • 2: VGA (640 × 480 pixels)
  • 3: SVGA (832 × 624 pixels)
  • 4: XGA (1024 × 768 pixels)
  • 5: UXGA (1600 × 1200 pixels)
Columns Factor

Lighting Intensity

2 Levels (\(N_{\text{Columns}} = 2\))

  • Column 1: Low Light

    Dim room lighting mimicking evening gameplay conditions

  • Column 2: High Light

    Bright ambient lighting producing glare risks on standard monitors

Dependent Variable (DV)

Game Performance

Continuous ratio metric

Number of Lives Used: Higher values indicate greater player error, fatigue, or visual hindrance during standard gaming scenarios.
Sample size per cell (\(n_i\)): 4
Total experimental cells (\(N_{\text{Cells}}\)): 10
Total participants (\(n\)): 40

Statistical Hypotheses for a 2 × 5 Factorial Design

1. Main Effect of Rows (Resolution)

Tests whether row means (\(M_{\text{Row}}\)) differ when averaged across both lighting conditions.

Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\)): \( \mu_{\text{Row 1}} = \mu_{\text{Row 2}} = \mu_{\text{Row 3}} = \mu_{\text{Row 4}} = \mu_{\text{Row 5}} \)
Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_a\)): At least one row mean differs from the others.

2. Main Effect of Columns (Lighting)

Tests whether column means (\(M_{\text{Column}}\)) differ when averaged across all 5 resolution types.

Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\)): \( \mu_{\text{Column 1}} = \mu_{\text{Column 2}} \)
Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_a\)): \( \mu_{\text{Column 1}} \neq \mu_{\text{Column 2}} \)

3. Interaction Effect (Rows × Columns)

Tests whether differences across cell means (\(M_{\text{Cell}}\)) equal the combined additive row and column effects.

Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\)): No interaction effect exists. Lighting differences are invariant across all display resolutions.
Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_a\)): A significant interaction exists. The effect of lighting differs across display resolutions.

In R, the colon operator (FactorA:FactorB) identifies the interaction between those factors. Here, the test statistic is F(4, 30) = 10.691 with a p value of p = 1.66e-05 (which is < .001).

1. The Golden Rule of Factorial Tables

Always inspect the interaction row before interpreting main effects. Because \(p < .05\), the main effects cannot be interpreted as universal or uniform statements.

2. Substantive Meaning

The difference in lives lost between Low Light and High Light is not constant across monitor resolutions; the effect of lighting is conditional on the level of resolution.

Step 1: Locate the Colon

Identify the Interaction Term

Scan down the table to find the term joined by a colon (Resolution:LightLevel). The degrees of freedom should always equal \((N_{\text{Rows}} - 1)(N_{\text{Columns}} - 1)\). Here, \((5 - 1)(2 - 1) = 4\).

Step 2: Check the F & Pr(>F)

Evaluate Significance

Check the Pr(>F) column. Because \(p = 1.66 \times 10^{-5} < .05\), the interaction is statistically significant. The probability of observing differences this large under the null hypothesis is less than 0.002%.

Step 3: Decide Next Analytical Action

Qualify Main Effects

When the interaction row is significant, you do not stop at reporting marginal main effects. You proceed directly to simple effects testing (e.g., via emmeans() or phia::testInteractions()) to test where the differences occur.

Complete Step by Step Hand Calculation Walkthrough

Follow the derivation using course lecture formulas from individual deviations all the way to ANOVA test statistics.

A. Total Sum of Squares (\(SS_{\text{Total}}\))

Lecture formula: \(\sum (X - M_{\text{grand}})^2\), summing the squared deviation of each individual observation from the grand mean (\(M_{\text{grand}} = 11.375\)) across all \(n = 40\) scores in the study:

\(SS_{\text{Total}} = \sum (X - M_{\text{grand}})^2\)
\(= (6 - 11.375)^2 + (12 - 11.375)^2 + (6 - 11.375)^2 + \dots + (11 - 11.375)^2\)
\(= (-5.375)^2 + (0.625)^2 + (-5.375)^2 + \dots + (-0.375)^2\)
\(= 28.890625 + 0.390625 + 28.890625 + \dots + 0.140625 = \mathbf{759.375}\)
B. Rows Sum of Squares (\(SS_{\text{Rows}}\))

Lecture formula: \(\sum n_i(M_{\text{Row}} - M_{\text{grand}})^2\), where \(n_i = 8\) scores per row, summed across all \(N_{\text{Rows}} = 5\) rows:

\(SS_{\text{Rows}} = \sum n_i (M_{\text{Row}} - M_{\text{grand}})^2\)
\(= 8 \cdot [(11.50 - 11.375)^2 + (13.00 - 11.375)^2 + (12.00 - 11.375)^2 + (11.25 - 11.375)^2 + (9.125 - 11.375)^2]\)
\(= 8 \cdot [0.015625 + 2.640625 + 0.390625 + 0.015625 + 5.0625] = 8 \cdot 8.125 = \mathbf{65.000}\)
C. Columns Sum of Squares (\(SS_{\text{Columns}}\))

Lecture formula: \(\sum n_i(M_{\text{Column}} - M_{\text{grand}})^2\), where \(n_i = 20\) scores per column, summed across both \(N_{\text{Columns}} = 2\) columns:

\(SS_{\text{Columns}} = \sum n_i (M_{\text{Column}} - M_{\text{grand}})^2\)
\(= 20 \cdot [(9.95 - 11.375)^2 + (12.80 - 11.375)^2] = 20 \cdot [(-1.425)^2 + (1.425)^2]\)
\(= 20 \cdot [2.030625 + 2.030625] = 20 \cdot 4.06125 = \mathbf{81.225}\)
D. Interaction Sum of Squares (\(SS_{\text{Interaction}}\))

Lecture formula: \(\sum [(M_{\text{Cell}} - M_{\text{grand}}) - (M_{\text{Row}} - M_{\text{grand}}) - (M_{\text{Column}} - M_{\text{grand}})]^2\)

\(SS_{\text{Interaction}} = \sum n_i [(M_{\text{Cell}} - M_{\text{grand}}) - (M_{\text{Row}} - M_{\text{grand}}) - (M_{\text{Column}} - M_{\text{grand}})]^2\)
Evaluating all 10 cells individually across the 5 rows with \(M_{\text{grand}} = 11.375\), \(M_{\text{Column 1}} = 9.95\), and \(M_{\text{Column 2}} = 12.80\):
Row 1: QVGA (\(M_{\text{Row 1}} = 11.50\))
Low Light (Cell 1,1): \(4 \cdot [(8.00 - 11.375) - (11.50 - 11.375) - (9.95 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(-3.375) - (0.125) - (-1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [-2.075]^2 = 4 \cdot 4.305625 = \mathbf{17.2225}\)
High Light (Cell 1,2): \(4 \cdot [(15.00 - 11.375) - (11.50 - 11.375) - (12.80 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(3.625) - (0.125) - (1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [2.075]^2 = 4 \cdot 4.305625 = \mathbf{17.2225}\)
Row 1 Subtotal: \(17.2225 + 17.2225 = 34.445\)
Row 2: VGA (\(M_{\text{Row 2}} = 13.00\))
Low Light (Cell 2,1): \(4 \cdot [(9.00 - 11.375) - (13.00 - 11.375) - (9.95 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(-2.375) - (1.625) - (-1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [-2.575]^2 = 4 \cdot 6.630625 = \mathbf{26.5225}\)
High Light (Cell 2,2): \(4 \cdot [(17.00 - 11.375) - (13.00 - 11.375) - (12.80 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(5.625) - (1.625) - (1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [2.575]^2 = 4 \cdot 6.630625 = \mathbf{26.5225}\)
Row 2 Subtotal: \(26.5225 + 26.5225 = 53.045\)
Row 3: SVGA (\(M_{\text{Row 3}} = 12.00\))
Low Light (Cell 3,1): \(4 \cdot [(8.00 - 11.375) - (12.00 - 11.375) - (9.95 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(-3.375) - (0.625) - (-1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [-2.575]^2 = 4 \cdot 6.630625 = \mathbf{26.5225}\)
High Light (Cell 3,2): \(4 \cdot [(16.00 - 11.375) - (12.00 - 11.375) - (12.80 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(4.625) - (0.625) - (1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [2.575]^2 = 4 \cdot 6.630625 = \mathbf{26.5225}\)
Row 3 Subtotal: \(26.5225 + 26.5225 = 53.045\)
Row 4: XGA (\(M_{\text{Row 4}} = 11.25\))
Low Light (Cell 4,1): \(4 \cdot [(14.50 - 11.375) - (11.25 - 11.375) - (9.95 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(3.125) - (-0.125) - (-1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [4.675]^2 = 4 \cdot 21.855625 = \mathbf{87.4225}\)
High Light (Cell 4,2): \(4 \cdot [(8.00 - 11.375) - (11.25 - 11.375) - (12.80 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(-3.375) - (-0.125) - (1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [-4.675]^2 = 4 \cdot 21.855625 = \mathbf{87.4225}\)
Row 4 Subtotal: \(87.4225 + 87.4225 = 174.845\)
Row 5: UXGA (\(M_{\text{Row 5}} = 9.125\))
Low Light (Cell 5,1): \(4 \cdot [(10.25 - 11.375) - (9.125 - 11.375) - (9.95 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(-1.125) - (-2.250) - (-1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [2.550]^2 = 4 \cdot 6.5025 = \mathbf{26.0100}\)
High Light (Cell 5,2): \(4 \cdot [(8.00 - 11.375) - (9.125 - 11.375) - (12.80 - 11.375)]^2\)
\(= 4 \cdot [(-3.375) - (-2.250) - (1.425)]^2 = 4 \cdot [-2.550]^2 = 4 \cdot 6.5025 = \mathbf{26.0100}\)
Row 5 Subtotal: \(26.0100 + 26.0100 = 52.020\)
\(SS_{\text{Interaction}} = 34.445 + 53.045 + 53.045 + 174.845 + 52.020 = \mathbf{367.400}\)
E. Within / Residual Sum of Squares (\(SS_{\text{Within}}\))

Lecture formula: \(\sum (X - M_{\text{Cell}})^2\), summing squared deviations of each observation from its own cell mean across all 10 cells:

\(SS_{\text{Within}} = \sum (X - M_{\text{Cell}})^2\)
\(= 24 + 24 + 30 + 30 + 24 + 36 + 37 + 26 + 6.75 + 20 = \mathbf{257.750}\)
\(df_{\text{Columns}}\)

\(N_{\text{Columns}} - 1 = 2 - 1 = \mathbf{1}\)

\(df_{\text{Rows}}\)

\(N_{\text{Rows}} - 1 = 5 - 1 = \mathbf{4}\)

\(df_{\text{Interaction}}\)

\((N_{\text{Rows}} - 1)(N_{\text{Columns}} - 1) = 4 \cdot 1 = \mathbf{4}\)

\(df_{\text{Within}}\)

\(n - (\# \text{ of cells}) = 40 - 10 = \mathbf{30}\)

Total degrees of freedom: \(df_{\text{Total}} = n - 1 = 40 - 1 = \mathbf{39}\)

Each effect Mean Square is computed by dividing its \(SS\) by its \(df\). The common error variance is \(MS_{\text{Within}}\):

\(MS_{\text{Within}} = \frac{SS_{\text{Within}}}{df_{\text{Within}}} = \frac{257.750}{30} = \mathbf{8.5917}\)
Between: Columns (Lighting)
\(MS_{\text{Columns}} = \frac{81.225}{1} = 81.225\)
\(F = \frac{81.225}{8.5917} = \mathbf{9.454}\)
\(p = .0045\)
Result: Significant (\(p < .01\))
Between: Rows (Resolution)
\(MS_{\text{Rows}} = \frac{65.000}{4} = 16.250\)
\(F = \frac{16.250}{8.5917} = \mathbf{1.891}\)
\(p = .138\)
Result: Not Significant
Interaction
\(MS_{\text{Interaction}} = \frac{367.400}{4} = 91.850\)
\(F = \frac{91.850}{8.5917} = \mathbf{10.691}\)
\(p = 1.66 \times 10^{-5}\)
Result: Significant (\(p < .001\))

In factorial ANOVA, partial eta squared represents the proportion of variance explained by a specific factor:

\(\eta_p^2 = \frac{SS_{\text{Effect}}}{SS_{\text{Effect}} + SS_{\text{Within}}}\)
\(\eta_p^2\) Columns (Lighting)
\( \frac{81.225}{81.225 + 257.750} = \mathbf{0.240}\)
\(\eta_p^2\) Rows (Resolution)
\( \frac{65.000}{65.000 + 257.750} = \mathbf{0.201}\)
\(\eta_p^2\) Interaction
\( \frac{367.400}{367.400 + 257.750} = \mathbf{0.588}\)

Formal APA 7th Results Section Paragraph

A 2 (Lighting: Low vs. High) × 5 (Resolution: QVGA, VGA, SVGA, XGA, UXGA) between subjects factorial ANOVA was conducted to assess differences in video game difficulty (measured in number of lives used). The main effect of graphics resolution was not statistically significant, \(F(4, 30) = 1.89, p = .138, \eta_p^2 = .201\). In contrast, the main effect of lighting intensity was statistically significant, \(F(1, 30) = 9.45, p = .004, \eta_p^2 = .240\), with players in high lighting consuming more lives (\(M = 12.80, SD = 4.76\)) than those in low lighting (\(M = 9.95, SD = 3.69\)). Crucially, this main effect was qualified by a statistically significant Resolution × Lighting interaction effect, \(F(4, 30) = 10.69, p < .001, \eta_p^2 = .588\). Because the interaction effect reached statistical significance in the ANOVA table, the main effect of lighting cannot be interpreted as uniform across screen types, and simple main effects analyses must be consulted to determine where lighting differences emerge.

A 2 (Study Method: Active Recall vs. Passive Review) × 2 (Environment: Quiet Library vs. Noisy Cafe) between subjects factorial ANOVA was conducted on exam scores. The Method × Environment interaction effect was not statistically significant, F(1, 76) = 0.21, p = .650. The main effect of environment was also not statistically significant, F(1, 76) = 2.40, p = .125. However, there was a statistically significant main effect of study method, F(1, 76) = 14.02, p < .001. Pairwise marginal comparisons using emmeans indicated that students utilizing active recall achieved significantly higher exam scores (M = 79.6, SE = 1.37) than students utilizing passive review (M = 72.4, SE = 1.37), t(76) = 3.74, p < .001. Because the interaction was not statistically significant, this main effect can be interpreted across environments without decomposing conditional simple effects.

Model Results Paragraph:

A 2 (Caffeine: Placebo vs. 200mg Caffeine) × 2 (Task Difficulty: Simple vs. Complex) between subjects factorial ANOVA was conducted on reaction times. The main effect of caffeine was statistically significant, F(1, 56) = 6.25, p = .015, as was the main effect of task difficulty, F(1, 56) = 32.67, p < .001. However, both main effects were qualified by a statistically significant Caffeine × Task Difficulty interaction effect, F(1, 56) = 11.17, p = .001. Analysis of simple effects using emmeans revealed that for simple tasks, participants receiving 200mg caffeine exhibited significantly faster reaction times (M = 473 ms, SE = 11.0) than those receiving placebo (M = 539 ms, SE = 11.0), t(56) = 4.13, p < .001. Conversely, for complex tasks, reaction times did not significantly differ between placebo (M = 597 ms, SE = 11.0) and 200mg caffeine conditions (M = 609 ms, SE = 11.0), t(56) = -0.78, p = .442.
