Interpretation of Omnibus Results

Because the observed significance level $p = .007$ is less than standard $\alpha = .05$, we reject the omnibus null hypothesis that all three group means are equal.

The Core Challenge: The omnibus $F$ test confirms that at least one group mean is significantly different from the others, but it cannot tell us:

  • Which specific means are significantly different from each other.
  • The direction of the differences (which specific dosage level produces superior attention performance).

Error Rates & Type I Error Inflation

When performing multiple statistical comparisons among means, the major challenge is the compounding inflation of Type I error. Researchers define error rates under two formal paradigms:

Individual Type I Error Rate

Also termed the error rate per comparison. This is the probability for a single individual test that the null hypothesis will be rejected when the null hypothesis is in fact true.

$\alpha_{PC} = \alpha \text{ (typically } .05 \text{)}$

Familywise Type I Error Rate

The probability for an entire family of statistical tests that at least one null hypothesis will be rejected when all null hypotheses are true.

$\alpha_{FW} = 1 - (1 - \alpha)^c$

Comparison Techniques & Decision Framework

A Priori (Planned Contrasts)

Differences between group means are predicted by prior theory before viewing sample data. Because empirical theory motivates the tests, they retain higher statistical power and usually permit less stringent corrections.

Post Hoc (Unplanned Comparisons)

Follow up examinations conducted after viewing empirical findings, exploring unexpected differences. Because tests are atheoretical and numerous, familywise Type I error inflation must be strictly controlled.

Technique When to Use Stringency Level
Linear Contrasts Specific planned hypotheses testing one group or a set of groups against another. Can be orthogonal (independent) or non orthogonal. Flexible. Options include testing at a stricter alpha, applying Bonferroni adjustments, or conducting fewer total contrasts.
Bonferroni Adjustment When performing a small number of planned comparisons to maintain exact familywise error limits ($\alpha / c$). Less stringent with very few comparisons, but becomes increasingly stringent as comparisons grow larger.
Tukey HSD When testing all possible pairwise combinations among means, either planned a priori or exploratory post hoc. Moderately stringent. Maintains excellent power balance compared to Scheffe while controlling familywise alpha.
Scheffe Method Post hoc complex linear combinations and exploratory data mining across many treatment conditions. Most stringent method available. Greatly reduces Type I error but increases the probability of Type II error.

Methodological Perspectives on Corrections

Standard Textbook Recommendation:

Argues that whether comparisons are planned or not, total volume of comparisons matters. Whenever conducting multiple contrasts ($c > 3$), familywise Type I error inflation corrections must be applied.

Keppel & Wickens (2004) Framework:

Argues planned comparisons motivated by formal prior empirical hypothesis do not require alpha penalties because prior grounding minimizes false discovery probability; adjustments should remain reserved for unexpected post hoc queries.

Multiple t Tests Formula Utilizing $MS_{error}$

The mathematical foundation of these comparison techniques replaces individual sample variances with the pooled error variance estimate ($MS_{error}$) from the omnibus ANOVA model.

Standard Independent t Test
$$t = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}$$

Relies solely on individual sample variances ($s_1^2, s_2^2$) from the two groups being tested, discarding information from the remaining sample groups.

Omnibus Error Replacement t Test
$$t = \frac{\bar{X}_i - \bar{X}_j}{\sqrt{\frac{MS_{error}}{n} + \frac{MS_{error}}{n}}}$$

Replaces isolated variances with $MS_{error}$ across all three conditions ($MS_{error} = 37$, $df_{error} = 24$), yielding substantially higher statistical power.

Assumptions & Decision Protocol

✓
Condition 1: Homogeneity of variance holds across conditions, allowing individual group variances to be pooled into omnibus $MS_{error}$.
✓
Condition 2: Equal sample sizes exist across each group ($n_1 = n_2 = n_3 = 9$).

Violation Protocol (Unequal Variances):

• If homogeneity is violated with equal $n$: Use individual group variances and evaluate $t$ on $2(n - 1)$ degrees of freedom.

• If homogeneity is violated with unequal $n$: Use individual group variances with the Welch Satterthwaite degrees of freedom adjustment.

Orthogonal Linear Contrasts Verification & Calculator

A linear combination $L$ is a contrast $\Psi$ when the sum of coefficients $\sum a_j = 0$. Orthogonal contrasts provide completely independent pieces of theoretical variance and satisfy specific mathematical properties:

Properties of Orthogonal Sets:
  • Exactly $k - 1$ contrasts can be established in a complete orthogonal set ($3 - 1 = 2$).
  • Coefficients within each contrast must sum to zero: $\sum a_{1j} = 0$ and $\sum a_{2j} = 0$.
  • Cross products of coefficients across contrasts must sum to zero: $\sum (a_{1j} \cdot a_{2j}) = 0$.
  • The contrast sums of squares partition treatment variance: $SS_{contrast 1} + SS_{contrast 2} = SS_{treatment} = 456$.
$$SS_{contrast} = \frac{n \cdot (\Psi)^2}{\sum a_j^2} \quad \text{where} \quad \Psi = \sum (a_j \cdot \bar{X}_j), \quad F = \frac{MS_{contrast}}{MS_{error}}, \quad t = \sqrt{F}$$

Tukey HSD & Critical Difference Procedure

Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference tests all pairwise combinations by adjusting the critical decision threshold rather than computing test statistics beyond the raw mean difference.

Critical Difference Formula

$$D_t = q \cdot \sqrt{\frac{MS_{error}}{n}}$$

• $q$: Studentized range statistic evaluated at $r = 3$ (number of groups) and $df_{error} = 24$.

• $MS_{error} = 37$, $n = 9$ (number of participants per group).

• Decision criterion: If $|\bar{X}_i - \bar{X}_j| > D_t$, the pair of means is statistically significantly different.

Formal APA Results Report & Guidelines

Below is the comprehensive APA format narrative summarizing the omnibus ANOVA findings and planned orthogonal linear contrasts:

The omnibus ANOVA indicated that there was a significant difference in accuracy on a sustained attention task with different amounts of caffeine, F(2, 24) = 6.16, p = .007, η² = .339. Planned orthogonal linear contrasts were conducted.

When comparing high caffeine intake to the combination of low caffeine intake and no caffeine intake, there was a significant difference, t(24) = 3.22, p = .004, such that the mean accuracy for high caffeine intake (M = 16.67, SD = 6.32) was significantly higher than the combination of low and no caffeine intake (M = 8.67, SD = 6.14).

Further, there was not a significant difference in accuracy when comparing no caffeine intake (M = 6.67, SD = 6.32) to low caffeine intake (M = 10.67, SD = 5.57), t(24) = 1.40, p = .18.

Bonferroni Adjustment Protocol

Because only two planned orthogonal linear contrasts were evaluated, familywise error remains bounded at $1 - (1 - .05)^2 = .0975$ (under $10\%$).

If a strict Bonferroni correction is preferred, the criterion is adjusted to $\alpha_{adj} = \frac{.05}{2} = .025$. Under this threshold, Contrast 1 remains significant ($p = .004 < .025$), while Contrast 2 remains not significant ($p = .18$). When adopting an adjusted alpha, researchers explicitly cite the new decision boundary in their report.
