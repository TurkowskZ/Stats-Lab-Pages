Lecture Formula Notation and Symbol Reference Guide

Aligned with Chapter 11 Lecture Slides: Structural model, deviations around means, and ANOVA table syntax

Master Legend

Counts and Structural Model

  • $k$: Total number of treatment groups or levels of the factor. Here $k = 3$.
  • $n_i$: Sample size within group $i$. When all groups are equal, every group has $n_i = 9$.
  • $n$: Total number of scores across all groups combined ($n = \sum n_i$) in the lecture df formulas ($df_{Total} = n - 1$, $df_{Within} = n - k$).
  • $X$ (or $X_{ij}$): Individual raw score of participant $i$ in group $j$.
  • $X_{ij} = \mu + (\mu_j - \mu) + (X_{ij} - \mu_j)$: Structural model where $\tau_j = (\mu_j - \mu)$ is the treatment effect and $\epsilon_{ij} = (X_{ij} - \mu_j)$ is individual error.

Means and Group Totals

  • $M_i$ (or $M$): Sample mean for a specific treatment group (e.g., $M_A, M_B, M_C$).
  • $M_{\text{Grand}}$: Grand Mean across all observations in the study ($M_{\text{Grand}} = \frac{\sum X}{n}$).
  • $(X - M_{\text{Grand}})$: Deviation of an individual score around the Grand Mean (Total variation component).
  • $(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})$: Deviation of a sample group mean around the Grand Mean (Treatment Effect component).
  • $(X - M)$: Deviation of an individual score around its own group sample mean (Error component).

Sums of Squares (SS)

  • $SS_{Total} = \sum^{scores}(X - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$: Sum of squared deviations of all scores around the Grand Mean.
  • $SS_{Effect} = \sum^{k} n_i(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$: Also written as $SS_{Between}$. Deviation of sample means around the Grand Mean, weighted by group size $n_i$.
  • $SS_{Error} = \sum^{all\ scores}(X - M)^2$: Also written as $SS_{Within}$. Deviation of each score around its respective group mean.
  • $SS_{Total} = SS_{Effect} + SS_{Error}$: Partitioning of total sum of squares into treatment effect and error.

MS, F Ratio, and Eta Squared

  • $df_{Between} = k - 1$: Numerator degrees of freedom ($df_{Effect}$).
  • $df_{Within} = n - k$: Denominator degrees of freedom ($df_{Error}$).
  • $s^2$ (or $MS$): Mean Square variance estimate ($MS_{Effect} = SS_{Effect}/df_{Effect}$ and $MS_{Error} = SS_{Error}/df_{Error}$).
  • $F = \frac{MS_{Effect}}{MS_{Error}} = \frac{MS_{Between}}{MS_{Within}}$: Ratio of between groups variance to within groups error variance ($\sigma^2_e$).
  • $\eta^2 = \frac{SS_{between}}{SS_{total}}$: Eta squared ($r$ family effect size), proportion of total variance accounted for by the treatment effect.

Grand Mean ($M_{\text{Grand}}$)

Unweighted Average Equals Overall Mean
Lecture Grand Mean Formula
$$M_{\text{Grand}} = \frac{\sum^{scores} X}{n} = \frac{M_1 + M_2 + \dots + M_k}{k}$$
Sum of all scores divided by total $n$:
$$M_{\text{Grand}} = \frac{39 + 48 + 57}{27} = \frac{144}{27} \approx 5.3333$$
Average of sample means verification: (4.3333 + 5.3333 + 6.3333) / 3 = 5.3333
Key equality principle: When $n_i$ is equal across all groups, $M_{\text{Grand}}$ is identical whether calculated by summing all raw $X$ scores and dividing by total $n$, or by averaging the group means $M_i$.
2

Grand Mean ($M_{\text{Grand}}$)

Weighted by Group Sample Size $n_i$
Lecture Grand Mean Formula
$$M_{\text{Grand}} = \frac{\sum^{scores} X}{n} = \frac{\sum^{k} n_i M_i}{n}$$
Sum of all scores divided by total $n$:
$$M_{\text{Grand}} = \frac{39 + 40 + 45}{24} = \frac{(9)(4.3333) + (8)(5.0000) + (7)(6.4286)}{24} = \frac{124}{24} \approx 5.1667$$
Notice: (4.3333 + 5.0000 + 6.4286) / 3 = 5.2539 (Unweighted average of means does not equal $M_{\text{Grand}}$)
Crucial difference: You must compute $M_{\text{Grand}}$ as the sum of all $X$ scores divided by total $n$ (or weight each $M_i$ by $n_i$). Taking a simple average of the three group means ignores the unequal group sizes.

Total Variance: $SS_{Total}$

All 27 Scores
Deviation of All Data Around the Grand Mean
$$SS_{Total} = \sum^{scores} (X - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$$
Summing squared deviations $(X - 5.333)^2$ across all 27 scores:
Group 1: $(1 - 5.333)^2 + (4 - 5.333)^2 + \dots + (7 - 5.333)^2 = 89.000$
Group 2: $(5 - 5.333)^2 + (2 - 5.333)^2 + \dots + (8 - 5.333)^2 = 62.000$
Group 3: $(3 - 5.333)^2 + (6 - 5.333)^2 + \dots + (9 - 5.333)^2 = 89.000$
$$SS_{Total} = 89.000 + 62.000 + 89.000 = 240.000$$

Measures the total squared deviation of every score $X$ around the Grand Mean ($M_{\text{Grand}} = 5.333$).

Total Variance: $SS_{Total}$

All 24 Scores
Deviation of All Data Around the Grand Mean
$$SS_{Total} = \sum^{scores} (X - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$$
Summing squared deviations $(X - 5.167)^2$ across all 24 scores:
Group 1 ($n_1=9$): $\sum (X - 5.167)^2 = 86.250$
Group 2 ($n_2=8$): $\sum (X - 5.167)^2 = 54.222$
Group 3 ($n_3=7$): $\sum (X - 5.167)^2 = 68.861$
$$SS_{Total} = 86.250 + 54.222 + 68.861 = 209.333$$

Uses the exact same formula $\sum^{scores}(X - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$ across all 24 available scores around $M_{\text{Grand}} = 5.167$.

4

Effect Variance: $SS_{Effect}$ ($SS_{Between}$)

Equal Weight $n_i = 9$
Deviation of Sample Means Around the Grand Mean
$$SS_{Effect} = \sum^{k} n_i(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$$
Step by step substitution ($n_1 = n_2 = n_3 = 9$, $M_{\text{Grand}} = 5.333$):
$SS_{Effect} = 9(4.333 - 5.333)^2 + 9(5.333 - 5.333)^2 + 9(6.333 - 5.333)^2$
$SS_{Effect} = 9(-1.000)^2 + 9(0.000)^2 + 9(1.000)^2$
$$SS_{Effect} = 9(1) + 9(0) + 9(1) = 18.000$$
Balanced design property: Because every group has the same sample size ($n_i = 9$), each squared treatment effect $(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$ is multiplied by the exact same constant $9$.
4

Effect Variance: $SS_{Effect}$ ($SS_{Between}$)

Weighted by Each Group's $n_i$
Deviation of Sample Means Around the Grand Mean
$$SS_{Effect} = \sum^{k} n_i(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$$
Step by step substitution ($n_1=9, n_2=8, n_3=7$, $M_{\text{Grand}} = 5.167$):
$SS_{Effect} = 9(4.333 - 5.167)^2 + 8(5.000 - 5.167)^2 + 7(6.429 - 5.167)^2$
$SS_{Effect} = 9(-0.8333)^2 + 8(-0.1667)^2 + 7(1.2619)^2$
$$SS_{Effect} = 6.250 + 0.222 + 11.146 = 17.619$$
Why $n_i$ is inside the summation: The lecture formula $\sum^{k} n_i(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$ already handles unequal sample sizes automatically because each group's squared deviation $(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$ is multiplied by its specific group size $n_i$.

Error Variance: $SS_{Error}$ ($SS_{Within}$)

Deviations Around $M_i$
Deviation of Scores Around the Sample Mean
$$SS_{Error} = \sum^{all\ scores} (X - M)^2$$
Group 1 ($M_1 = 4.333$): $\sum (X - 4.333)^2 = 80.000$
Group 2 ($M_2 = 5.333$): $\sum (X - 5.333)^2 = 62.000$
Group 3 ($M_3 = 6.333$): $\sum (X - 6.333)^2 = 80.000$
$$SS_{Error} = 80.000 + 62.000 + 80.000 = 222.000$$
Check: $SS_{Total} - SS_{Effect} = 240.000 - 18.000 = 222.000$

Measures variability of scores within the same condition around their own group mean $M$.

5

Error Variance: $SS_{Error}$ ($SS_{Within}$)

Deviations Around $M_i$
Deviation of Scores Around the Sample Mean
$$SS_{Error} = \sum^{all\ scores} (X - M)^2$$
Group 1 ($n_1=9, M_1 = 4.333$): $\sum (X - 4.333)^2 = 80.000$
Group 2 ($n_2=8, M_2 = 5.000$): $\sum (X - 5.000)^2 = 54.000$
Group 3 ($n_3=7, M_3 = 6.429$): $\sum (X - 6.429)^2 = 57.714$
$$SS_{Error} = 80.000 + 54.000 + 57.714 = 191.714$$
Check: $SS_{Total} - SS_{Effect} = 209.333 - 17.619 = 191.714$

Each participant's score $X$ is subtracted from its own group mean $M$, squared, and summed across all 24 scores.

Total $n = 27, k = 3$
$df_{Effect}$ ($df_{Between}$) $k - 1 = 3 - 1$ 2
$df_{Error}$ ($df_{Within}$) $n - k = 27 - 3$ 24
$df_{Total}$ $n - 1 = 27 - 1$ 26
In the lecture slides, $n$ in the $df$ formulas stands for total sample size across all groups ($n = 27$), so $df_{Error} = n - k = 27 - 3 = 24$.

Effect Size (Eta squared, $\eta^2$):
$$\eta^2 = \frac{SS_{between}}{SS_{total}} = \frac{MS_{between} \cdot df_{between}}{(MS_{between} \cdot df_{between}) + (MS_{within} \cdot df_{within})} = \frac{18.000}{240.000} = 0.075$$
Statistical Inference: Since computed $F(0.973)$ is less extreme than $F_{critical}(2, 24) = 3.403$, we fail to reject $H_0$ ($\mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu_3$).

ANOVA Assumptions and Methodological Properties

Equal Sample Properties
1. Robustness to Homogeneity of Variance ($\sigma^2_1 = \sigma^2_2 = \dots = \sigma^2_k = \sigma^2_e$)

As noted in the lecture slides, if you have equal group sizes ($n_i$), ANOVA is generally resilient ("robust") to moderate violations of both normality and homogeneity of variance.

2. Direct Estimation of $MS_{Effect}$ from Sample Mean Variance ($s^2_M$)

With equal sample sizes $n_i$, the central limit theorem relation from the lecture slides holds directly with a single $n_i$:

$$MS_{Between} = n_i \cdot s^2_M$$
3. Unweighted Pooling of Sample Variances

When all groups have the same $n_i$, $MS_{Within}$ ($\sigma^2_e$) is simply the unweighted arithmetic average of the individual group variances:

$$MS_{Within} = \frac{s^2_{group\ 1} + s^2_{group\ 2} + \dots + s^2_{group\ k}}{k}$$

ANOVA Assumptions and Methodological Properties

Unequal Sample Properties
1. Vulnerability to Homoscedasticity Violations (Levene's Test & Welch's ANOVA)

With unequal group sizes, standard ANOVA is not robust to violations of homogeneity of variance. If Levene's test is significant ($p < .05$), you must use Welch's ANOVA as specified in the lecture slides.

2. Group Size Weighting in $SS_{Effect}$

Because $n_i$ varies across groups, you cannot multiply the variance of sample means by a single $n_i$. You must weight each group's squared deviation by its own $n_i$:

$$SS_{Effect} = \sum^{k} n_i(M_i - M_{\text{Grand}})^2$$
3. Degrees of Freedom Weighting for $MS_{Within}$

$MS_{Within}$ is no longer a simple average of the group variances $s^2_{group\ i}$; instead, summing $\sum(X - M)^2$ across all scores weights each group's variance by its degrees of freedom $(n_i - 1)$ divided by $df_{Within} = n - k$.
