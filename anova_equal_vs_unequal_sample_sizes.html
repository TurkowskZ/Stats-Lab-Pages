Mathematical Notation and Symbol Reference Guide

Definitions for indices, dot notation sums, squared totals, and test statistics

Master Legend

Counts and Indices

  • $k$: Total number of treatment groups or levels. Here $k = 3$.
  • $j$: Index denoting the specific treatment column ($j = 1, 2, \dots, k$).
  • $i$: Index denoting the individual observation inside group $j$.
  • $n$: Sample size per group when every group contains equal counts.
  • $n_j$: Sample size belonging specifically to group $j$ in unequal designs.
  • $N$: Grand sample size across all conditions combined, $N = \sum n_j$.

Totals, Squared Totals, Dot Notation

  • $Y_{ij}$: Individual raw score of subject $i$ inside treatment condition $j$.
  • $T_j$: Total sum of observations inside group $j$, calculated as $\sum_{i=1}^{n_j} Y_{ij}$.
  • $T_j^2$: Square of an individual group total, multiplying $T_j$ by itself.
  • $T_{\cdot\cdot}$ or $M_{\text{Grand}}$: Grand total sum across every score. The two dots indicate summation over both index $i$ and index $j$.
  • $T_{\cdot\cdot}^2$: Square of the grand total sum across all participants. You sum all raw scores first, then square that resulting single number.
  • $\frac{T_{\cdot\cdot}^2}{N}$: Correction factor term. It equals $N$ multiplied by the squared grand mean, subtracting baseline elevation from sums of squares.

Means and Sums of Squares

  • $\bar{Y}_j$: Arithmetic mean of treatment group $j$, equal to $\frac{T_j}{n_j}$.
  • $\bar{Y}_{\cdot\cdot}$: Overall grand mean. The bar denotes an average while the two dots indicate averaging across all subjects and groups.
  • $\sum Y_{ij}^2$: Sum of each raw score squared before adding. Every individual observation is squared first, then summed.
  • $SS_{\text{total}}$: Total sum of squares reflecting overall dispersion across all $N$ scores.
  • $SS_{\text{between}}$: Sum of squares between groups capturing treatment effects among conditions.
  • $SS_{\text{within}}$: Sum of squares within groups measuring pooled experimental error or noise.

Variance and Test Statistics

  • $df$: Degrees of freedom representing independent pieces of information.
  • $MS$: Mean square variance estimate, computed as $SS$ divided by corresponding $df$.
  • $F$: Test statistic ratio, calculated as $MS_{\text{between}}$ divided by $MS_{\text{within}}$.
  • $p$ value: Probability of obtaining an $F$ statistic at least this extreme under a true null hypothesis.
  • $\alpha$: Significance threshold criterion, standardly set at 0.05.
  • $q$: Studentized range statistic utilized during post hoc multiple pairwise comparisons.

2

Grand Mean (Overall Mean)

Unweighted Average of Group Means
Theoretical Formulation
$$\bar{Y}_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k} \bar{Y}_j = \frac{T_{\cdot\cdot}}{N}$$
Simple arithmetic average of means:
$$\bar{Y}_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{4.3333 + 5.3333 + 6.3333}{3} = \frac{16.000}{3} \approx 5.3333$$
Grand total verification: T.. / N = 144 / 27 = 5.3333
Key equality principle: In a balanced design, the grand mean is identical whether calculated as the average of group means or as the grand total divided by N. Every condition carries an identical weight of 33.33 percent.
2

Grand Mean (Overall Mean)

Sample Size Weighted Average
Theoretical Formulation
$$\bar{Y}_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k} n_j \bar{Y}_j}{\sum n_j} = \frac{T_{\cdot\cdot}}{N}$$
Sample weighted calculation:
$$\bar{Y}_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{(9)(4.3333) + (8)(5.0000) + (7)(6.4286)}{24} = \frac{124}{24} \approx 5.1667$$
Notice: (4.3333 + 5.0000 + 6.4286) / 3 = 5.2539 (Incorrect unweighted value)
Crucial difference: An unweighted mean of group means is mathematically invalid for unbalanced data. Group 1 carries 9/24 weight while Group 3 carries only 7/24 weight, shifting the grand mean toward the larger group.

Sum of Squares Total (SST)

All 27 Scores
Total Sum of Squares Formula
$$SS_{\text{total}} = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_{ij}^2 - \frac{T_{\cdot\cdot}^2}{N}$$
Sum of squared observations: 249 + 318 + 441 = 1008
Correction factor: T..² / N = (144)² / 27 = 20736 / 27 = 768.000
$$SS_{\text{total}} = 1008 - 768.000 = 240.000$$

Total variation represents all deviation of individual scores around the common grand mean 5.333.

Sum of Squares Between Groups (SSB)

Constant Factor n = 9
Definitional and Computational Formulas
$$SS_{\text{between}} = n \sum_{j=1}^{k} (\bar{Y}_j - \bar{Y}_{\cdot\cdot})^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k} T_j^2 - \frac{T_{\cdot\cdot}^2}{N}$$
Method A (Factoring constant n):
SSB = 9 × [ (4.333 - 5.333)² + (5.333 - 5.333)² + (6.333 - 5.333)² ]
SSB = 9 × [ (-1.000)² + (0.000)² + (1.000)² ] = 9 × [ 1 + 0 + 1 ] = 18.000
Method B (Computational formula using group totals squared T_j²):
$$\frac{39^2 + 48^2 + 57^2}{9} - \frac{144^2}{27} = \frac{1521 + 2304 + 3249}{9} - 768 = \frac{7074}{9} - 768 = \mathbf{18.000}$$
Algebraic elegance: Because every condition has nine items, n pulls cleanly outside the summation sign, and all squared group totals divide by one shared denominator.
4

Sum of Squares Between Groups (SSB)

Individual Weighting by n_j
Definitional and Computational Formulas
$$SS_{\text{between}} = \sum_{j=1}^{k} n_j (\bar{Y}_j - \bar{Y}_{\cdot\cdot})^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{T_j^2}{n_j} - \frac{T_{\cdot\cdot}^2}{N}$$
Method A (Individually weighted terms):
SSB = 9(4.333 - 5.167)² + 8(5.000 - 5.167)² + 7(6.429 - 5.167)²
SSB = 9(-0.8333)² + 8(-0.1667)² + 7(1.2619)² = 6.250 + 0.222 + 11.146 = 17.619
Method B (Computational formula dividing each T_j² by its own n_j):
$$\left(\frac{39^2}{9} + \frac{40^2}{8} + \frac{45^2}{7}\right) - \frac{124^2}{24} = \left(169 + 200 + 289.286\right) - 640.667 = \mathbf{17.619}$$
Mandatory individual division: You cannot factor out sample size. Each group total squared must divide by its own specific group size: 39²/9, 40²/8, and 45²/7.
5

Sum of Squares Within Groups (SSW / SSE)

Pooled Group Errors
Within Groups Error Formula
$$SS_{\text{within}} = SS_{\text{total}} - SS_{\text{between}} = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{ij} - \bar{Y}_j)^2$$
Subtractive method: 240.000 - 18.000 = 222.000
Sum of separate group variations:
SS₁ = 249 - (39²/9) = 249 - 169 = 80.000
SS₂ = 318 - (48²/9) = 318 - 256 = 62.000
SS₃ = 441 - (57²/9) = 441 - 361 = 80.000
SSW = 80 + 62 + 80 = 222.000

Measures intrinsic random variation among subjects receiving the exact same caffeine dosage.

5

Sum of Squares Within Groups (SSW / SSE)

Pooled Group Errors
Within Groups Error Formula
$$SS_{\text{within}} = SS_{\text{total}} - SS_{\text{between}} = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n_j}(Y_{ij} - \bar{Y}_j)^2$$
Subtractive method: 209.333 - 17.619 = 191.714
Sum of separate group variations:
SS₁ = 249 - (39²/9) = 249 - 169.000 = 80.000
SS₂ = 254 - (40²/8) = 254 - 200.000 = 54.000
SS₃ = 347 - (45²/7) = 347 - 289.286 = 57.714
SSW = 80 + 54 + 57.714 = 191.714

Group sum of squares calculation uses the specific sample size for each column when squaring sums.

Methodological Strengths and Assumptions

Equal Sample Properties
1. Robustness Against Variance Inequality

Equal group allocations afford maximal resilience against violations of homoscedasticity. Even if one population variance is two or three times greater, the actual Type I error rate remains tightly anchored near the nominal 0.05 level.

2. Classical Tukey Honestly Significant Difference

Pairwise multiple comparisons use the original Tukey test statistic without modification because the standard error term contains one fixed denominator:

$$q = \frac{\bar{Y}_A - \bar{Y}_B}{\sqrt{\frac{MS_{\text{within}}}{n}}}$$
3. Statistical Power Maximization

Dividing available participants evenly among levels delivers the greatest statistical test power for detecting differences under a constant total participant sample budget.

9

Methodological Strengths and Assumptions

Unequal Sample Properties
1. Vulnerability to Variance Violations

Unequal sample counts make the test sensitive to heteroscedasticity. If smaller groups carry larger variances, the true Type I error rate inflates above 0.05 (producing false positives). If larger groups possess higher variance, the test becomes overly conservative.

2. Mandatory Tukey Kramer Adjustment

Standard Tukey HSD cannot be utilized directly. The Tukey Kramer modification adjusts the standard error using the harmonic mean of the two specific group sizes being contrasted:

$$q = \frac{\bar{Y}_A - \bar{Y}_B}{\sqrt{\frac{MS_{\text{within}}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n_A} + \frac{1}{n_B}\right)}}$$
3. Reduced Effective Sample Size

Unbalanced designs suffer power degradation because statistical precision is constrained by the smallest group in any pairwise contrast.
